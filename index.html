01 · Introduction

The Problem We Solve

Pneumonia kills 2.5 million people yearly, disproportionately affecting children under 5 in developing nations where radiologist expertise is scarce.

🫁
Global Burden

Pneumonia is a lung parenchyma infection responsible for approximately 2.5 million deaths annually worldwide. Infants younger than five years old and elderly populations are disproportionately affected, especially in developing countries with limited access to specialist radiologists.

📊
Diagnostic Challenge

Radiography remains the most common and cost-effective diagnostic tool, but its interpretation demands expertise and is highly subjective. The shortage of skilled radiologists in low-income regions creates a critical gap in early detection and treatment initiation.

🔬
Prior Art Gap

Wang et al.'s ChestX-ray14 achieved AUC 0.70–0.86 with vanilla cross-entropy, and CheXNet outperformed radiologists — but both lacked spatial attention modules. No prior work systematically combined Focal Loss with attention-based DenseNet for pneumonia detection.

✨
Our Innovation

We integrate CBAM sequential attention into DenseNet121, apply Focal Loss (α=0.75, γ=2.0) for class imbalance, use a two-phase progressive unfreezing strategy, and provide Grad-CAM explainability — all within a lightweight 8M-parameter model deployable on CPU hardware.

DenseNet121 Backbone

Each layer ℓ receives concatenated feature maps of all preceding layers, promoting feature reuse and alleviating vanishing gradients.

Dense Connection Formula
xₗ = Hₗ([x₀, x₁, ..., xₗ₋₁])
Final output: 7×7×1024 feature tensor from 224×224 input
Parameters: 7.98M (vs ResNet50's 25.6M)
Pretrained: ImageNet weights, progressive unfreezing

CBAM Attention Module

Sequential channel then spatial attention applied to F ∈ ℝ^(7×7×1024), enabling the model to focus on diagnostically relevant lung regions.

Channel Attention (r=16)
Mc = σ(MLP(AvgPool(F)) + MLP(MaxPool(F))) F' = Mc ⊗ F
Spatial Attention (7×7 conv)
Ms = σ(f^7×7([AvgPool(F'); MaxPool(F')])) F'' = Ms ⊗ F'

Focal Loss Function

Addresses 2.88× class imbalance by down-weighting easy examples and focusing learning on hard boundary cases — the most clinically consequential errors.

Focal Loss Formula
ℒFL(pt) = -αt(1-pt)^γ log(pt)
α = 0.75
NORMAL class weight
γ = 2.0
Focusing parameter

Classification Head

The attended tensor F'' passes through a carefully regularized classification head designed to prevent overfitting.

04 · Training Strategy

Two-Phase Training

Progressive unfreezing preserves ImageNet representations while adapting the model to the chest radiograph domain without catastrophic forgetting.

Phase 01 · 25 Epochs

🔒 Backbone Frozen

All DenseNet121 parameters frozen. Only CBAM and classification head are trained, allowing the new modules to converge without disrupting pre-trained representations.

OptimizerAdam
Learning Rate1×10⁻³

Phase 02 · 20 Epochs

🔓 Partial Unfreezing

DenseBlock4 and norm5 unfrozen for domain-specific fine-tuning. Shallower layers remain frozen to prevent catastrophic forgetting of low-level features.

06 · Explainability

Grad-CAM Visual Explanations

Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping visualises which lung regions drive predictions, providing physician-interpretable transparency essential for clinical trust.

How Grad-CAM Works

Gradients of the predicted class score are backpropagated to the final CBAM-modified feature map. The resulting heatmaps highlight regions with strong positive influence on the prediction — red/yellow = high attention, blue/green = low attention.

✓ Clinically Plausible

Correctly Predicted Pneumonia

High-intensity red/yellow activations localise bilateral lower-lobe consolidations and peri-hilar regions — radiologically consistent sites for community-acquired pneumonia. The right lower lobe is most frequently activated.

⚠ Boundary Case

False Positive (FP=122)

Diffuse activation without focal consolidation. Peri-hilar vascular markings in normal X-rays can resemble early interstitial pneumonia, leading the model to incorrectly predict pneumonia (26.1% FP rate).

○ Correct Normal

Correctly Predicted Normal

Absence of focal high-intensity activation in the lung parenchyma confirms the model correctly identifies no pathological consolidations. Only cool blue/green hues are present, confirming a healthy radiograph.

False Negative Analysis (FN=30, 3.8%)

The 30 false negatives (3.8% miss rate) exhibit a characteristic pattern: Grad-CAM activations appear near peripheral image regions or radiograph labelling markers (e.g., letter "R"), rather than the lung parenchyma. This indicates the model is responding to confounding artefacts rather than genuine pathology.

Root cause: Anatomically irrelevant responses consistent with confounding artefacts. Not genuine model failure — rather, dataset-specific image metadata influences.

Clinical impact: Physicians reviewing positive cases in subsequent steps will catch remaining cases, making 3.8% miss rate acceptable for screening.

08 · Conclusion

Summary & Future Work

Seven mutually reinforcing contributions establish a new state-of-the-art for lightweight, explainable pneumonia detection from chest X-rays.

Key Contributions

  • 1 CBAM sequential attention within DenseNet121 for binary chest X-ray classification
  • 2 Dual-level class imbalance: Focal Loss (α=0.75, γ=2.0) + WeightedRandomSampler
  • 3 Two-phase progressive unfreezing preserving ImageNet pre-trained features
  • 4 Threshold optimisation via Youden Index for clinically validated operating point
  • 5 Parameter efficiency: superior AUC at 31% of ResNet50 parameter cost (8M vs 25.6M)
  • 6 Structured Grad-CAM failure-mode taxonomy (3 clinically relevant categories)
  • 7 Rigorous multi-baseline comparative evaluation under identical experimental settings
Limitations
  • ▸Dataset limited to paediatric patients (1–5 years) from one Chinese institution
  • ▸Binary classification only; no bacterial vs viral distinction
  • ▸CPU hardware constrains batch size and hyperparameter exploration
  • ▸Grad-CAM provides coarse spatial localisation without pixel-wise segmentation

Future Directions

▸

Multi-institutional validation on adult and ethnically diverse cohorts to improve generalisability

▸

Multi-class extension to bacterial pneumonia, viral pneumonia, and COVID-19 pneumonitis

▸

Vision Transformer (ViT) and hybrid CNN-ViT backbone evaluation

▸

Clinical augmentation with auxiliary variables: age, SpO₂, symptom duration

▸

GPU-based ensemble learning for further performance improvements

▸

ONNX/TensorRT deployment for mobile and edge device applications

▸

Grad-CAM++ and attention rollout for higher-resolution localisation maps
